Why this addendum exists

Phase 13.2 and its addendum are closed: the three-channel decomposition holds, asset_delivery_error_mw is correctly named and correctly participates in frequency forcing, and B1b demonstrated channel independence exactly — asset_delivery_error_mw = 0.000000000 while frequency_forcing_mw = 1.000000000 on a load-model fault.

B1b also produced a result that changes the order of work: a 1 MW surplus drove frequency to 53.125 Hz in a single tick. That is a 6.25% excursion, well past any realistic over-frequency trip threshold.

The arithmetic is correct for the fixture. Working back through the swing equation, Δf = 1 / (2 × 4.0 × S_base) × 50 × 5.0 reaching +3.125 Hz implies S_base ≈ 2 MW, which is the sum of turbine ratings in that fixture. The test is valid and asserts the right property.

What it exposes is that frequency is being integrated at the 5-second dispatch cadence. Real frequency dynamics play out over hundreds of milliseconds. A 5-second Euler step can leap past a trip threshold entirely, and any droop response added later will act on a signal that has already moved several Hz. That has to be addressed before the droop loop, not after — otherwise the droop controller is being tuned against a numerically broken plant.

Constraint: no LLM, external API, or network dependency. All computation local, seeded, deterministic. All work targets core/simulation_core.py and the live engine path. Do not modify gridsignal_logger.py.

Phase 13.3b-0 — Decouple frequency integration from the dispatch tick

New, and blocking 13.3b.

The problem

TICK_INTERVAL_SIM_SECONDS = 5.0 is traced to Section 3.1 and is the intended dispatch evaluation cadence. That is defensible for dispatch: the turbine ramps at 0.2 MW/s and nothing useful changes faster.

It is not defensible for frequency. With H = 4.0 s, the electromechanical response has a time constant on the order of seconds, and a 5-second explicit Euler step is coarser than the dynamics it is integrating. Two consequences:

Frequency can jump past a relay threshold between samples, so protection logic can never see the crossing.
A proportional droop controller with a 5-second sample period against a 4-second inertia constant sits close to the regime where discrete-time control goes unstable.
Requirements
Integrate the swing equation on a sub-step: FREQUENCY_SUBSTEP_S, default 0.1 s, so one dispatch tick performs 50 sub-steps.
Forcing terms (frequency_forcing_mw, asset_delivery_error_mw) are held constant across the sub-steps within a tick. They are only recomputed at dispatch cadence, which is correct — dispatch genuinely does not update faster.
frequency_hz in TickResult reports the value at the end of the tick. Add frequency_min_hz and frequency_max_hz over the sub-steps within the tick, so an excursion that occurs and recovers between broadcasts is still visible.
Make the substep configurable and document why it differs from the dispatch cadence.
Acceptance criteria
#	Criterion	Threshold
N1	Same scenario at substep 0.1 s vs 0.01 s	end-of-tick frequency_hz differs by < 1%
N2	B1b re-run with sub-stepping	frequency trajectory smooth; no single-sample jump > 0.5 Hz
N3	frequency_min_hz / frequency_max_hz populated	reflect the intra-tick extremes, not the endpoint
N4	Forcing terms constant within a tick	verified by code review — they must not be recomputed per sub-step

N1 is the convergence test. If halving the substep materially changes the answer, the substep is still too coarse.

Phase 13.3b — Primary frequency response

Supersedes the 13.3b in Addendum A. Requirements are unchanged; the acceptance criteria gain a stability test.

governor_droop = 0.04 exists on SiteConfig and is set in test fixtures but is never read in simulation_core.py. The swing equation is a pure integrator with no restoring force, so frequency never returns to nominal even after an imbalance clears. B1b's 53.125 Hz would keep climbing on the next tick.

Requirements
Read governor_droop inside evaluate_tick().
Droop characteristic: ΔP = −Δf / (droop × f_nominal) × P_rated.
Droop response is subject to existing ramp limits (r_asset_up 0.2 MW/s, r_asset_down 0.15 MW/s). Primary response cannot exceed what the machine can physically deliver.
Governor deadband, default ±0.02 Hz.
The droop command enters the turbine setpoint path, so asset_delivery_error_mw continues to measure genuine delivery shortfall.
Acceptance criteria
#	Criterion	Threshold
I3	Islanded, sustained imbalance	frequency settles at a steady-state offset per the droop characteristic, not unbounded drift
I6	Clear an injected imbalance, islanded	frequency returns to within 0.05 Hz of nominal
I7	Island transition	accumulated frequency state preserved, not overwritten
I8	Droop response vs ΔP = −Δf / (droop × f_nominal) × P_rated	within ±10%
I9	Frequency within deadband	no turbine output change
I10	Droop command exceeding ramp limit	clamps at the limit; binding_constraint populated
I11	Closed-loop stability sweep	no divergence or sustained oscillation across H ∈ {2, 4, 8} s and substep ∈ {0.01, 0.1} s

I6 cannot pass against a pure integrator — it is the criterion that catches the missing restoring force.

I7 addresses line 878. The grid-connected branch overwrites state._frequency_hz with nominal every tick, discarding integration history, so re-islanding always starts clean. Hold the accumulated value and let it converge under droop.

I11 is new. If the loop oscillates or diverges, report it rather than silently reducing the droop gain — instability is evidence that the substep from 13.3b-0 is still too coarse, not that the gain is wrong.

Re-check B1b after this lands

With droop active and realistic asset ratings, the frequency excursion in B1b will be much smaller. Confirm the assertion frequency_hz > 50.0 still discriminates against noise, and tighten it to a specific expected value if not.
