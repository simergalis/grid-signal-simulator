In the context of a data centre like the one GridSignal simulates, a larger BESS buys you five concrete things:

1. Longer bridging before turbines must commit The GCC starts a gas turbine when turbine utilisation exceeds the commit threshold. If BESS can cover the gap for longer, the turbines stay offline (or cold standby) longer — saving fuel, wear, and startup emissions. A 5 MWh battery bridges a 5 MW shortfall for one hour; a 20 MWh battery bridges the same gap for four.

2. Absorb larger step-loads without frequency excursions When a rack of GPUs powers up, it creates an instantaneous demand step that turbines cannot match in time (they need 45 seconds to ramp). BESS responds in milliseconds. A larger rated-MW battery can absorb a bigger step — meaning you can admit larger GPU jobs without tripping the power cap or risking a frequency dip.

3. N-1 survivability with fewer committed turbines The GCC's N-1 reserve rule is turbine-only — it ignores BESS. But in practice, a large BESS with plenty of SoC provides a real-world safety cushion while fewer turbines are on bus. Operators can configure a smaller turbine fleet knowing the battery can ride through a single unit trip long enough for the next unit to come online.

4. More solar absorption, less curtailment Solar output fluctuates. A small battery fills up quickly when solar peaks and must be curtailed. A larger battery can absorb the full midday solar peak and discharge it into the evening ramp, increasing the fraction of renewable energy that actually reaches the load.

5. Anchor reserve stays cheap The grid-forming BESS unit holds p_anchor_reserve_mw permanently withheld for frequency regulation. On a small battery that reserve is a large fraction of usable capacity; on a large battery it's a small fraction, so more of the nameplate capacity is available for actual bridging.

The trade-off: a larger BESS costs more capex and has a higher anchor-reserve absolute MW commitment (the reserve scales with the config, not proportionally). In GridSignal you can explore this directly — increase rated MW/MWh via the BESS Config widget in the BATTERY panel and watch whether the GCC needs to commit a third turbine during the step-load sequence.
