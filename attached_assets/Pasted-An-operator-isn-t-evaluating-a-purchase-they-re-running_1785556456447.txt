An operator isn't evaluating a purchase; they're running a plant. A control-room panel answers three questions: is something coming, how long have I got, and is it handled?

Everything about the competitor is irrelevant at the desk.

Four states

At rest — quiet

NEXT LOAD EVENT
───────────────
None pending

Scheduler queue clear.
Notice on next event: 30–60 s

Tells the operator what to expect. No large number, because nothing is happening.

Counting down — the working state

INCOMING LOAD
─────────────
     23 s
until racks reach full draw

+20.0 MW predicted
Turbine ramping · BESS armed
Nothing required

Nothing required is the most valuable line on the panel. It's what an operator is actually checking for.

Reserve short — the exception

INCOMING LOAD — RESERVE SHORT
─────────────────────────────
     23 s
until racks reach full draw

+20.0 MW predicted
9.0 MW will be uncovered
         [ Acknowledge ]

Same layout, different verdict line and an action. The operator's eye lands in the same place whether things are fine or not.

Landed — confirmation, then quiet

LOAD LANDED
───────────
Staged 45 s ahead of arrival
Turbine 19.96 MW · BESS standby

Hold ~30 s, then return to the at-rest state.

Two things this fixes

The 0 s ambiguity disappears — the countdown reaching zero now says LOAD LANDED, which is an event, not a measurement.

And the panel stops being an argument. "That gap is the product" is a claim about a vendor. "Staged 45 s ahead of arrival" is a statement about the plant, and it's more persuasive to anyone technical precisely because it isn't trying to persuade.

Where the pitch copy belongs

Not deleted — moved. The "How it works" topology explainer is exactly the right home: it's onboarding, opened deliberately, and never sits in front of someone doing a job. That's where a sentence about power-sensor systems learning too late earns its place.

Keep the operating console describing the plant. Keep the explainer explaining the product.

One visual note

The panel is currently a large amber box occupying a quarter of the plant band while reading 0 s. Under ISA-101 discipline it should be muted grey at rest and gain colour only when counting down or short on reserve — amber for an active countdown, red for uncovered shortfall. A permanently-amber panel trains operators to stop seeing amber.
