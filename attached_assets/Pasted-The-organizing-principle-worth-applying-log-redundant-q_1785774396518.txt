The organizing principle worth applying: **log redundant quantities so invariants become checkable**. Every defect I found was diagnosable only because two channels happened to constrain each other (SOC vs. power integral), or was *undiagnosable* because nothing constrained the channel (terminal voltage, inlet temp). Deliberate redundancy turns debugging from inference into assertion.

## Tier 1 — close the power balance

This is the highest-value addition by a wide margin. Right now generation moved +139.6 MW net and nothing in the file accounts for where it went.

| Variable | Why |
|---|---|
| `site_total_load_mw` | Measured at the PCC or main switchgear |
| `it_load_mw` | IT critical load, separated from mechanical |
| `mechanical_load_mw` | Cooling, pumps, fans — the other half of PUE |
| `grid_import_mw` | Signed; plus an `islanded` boolean |
| `balance_residual_mw` | **Log the residual explicitly** — don't make the analyst compute it |

That last row is the trick. Generation − load − losses should be ~0. Logging the residual as its own column means a model bug announces itself instead of hiding, and it becomes a one-line assertion in your acceptance matrix: `abs(balance_residual_mw) < tolerance` on every tick.

## Tier 2 — make the product visible

The forecast engine is entirely absent from its own telemetry. You cannot currently compute forecast error, lead time, or NFR-2 compliance.

**Forecast record** — logged *as issued*, never recomputed:
- `forecast_issued_ts`, `forecast_target_ts`, `forecast_mw`
- `forecast_confidence` plus explicit interval bounds
- `forecast_error_mw` (signed, joined after the target time passes)
- `model_version`, `profile_library_version`, `calibration_version`

Recomputing a forecast during analysis destroys the audit trail — you have to capture what the engine actually said, when it said it.

**Latency chain** — five timestamps, because NFR-2 is a budget and budgets need line items:

`signal_ingest_ts` → `forecast_complete_ts` → `command_issued_ts` → `command_ack_ts` → `asset_response_ts`

Without the split you know only whether you hit 2 seconds, not which stage ate it.

**Setpoint/measurement pairs for every controllable asset.** This is the single most important omission in the current log. `bess_power_mw` is ambiguous between commanded and measured, which is exactly why the −50.000000 rail behavior was undiagnosable — I couldn't tell whether the clamp was in the controller or the plant model. Log `bess_setpoint_mw` and `bess_measured_mw` separately, and the same for the turbine. Controller bugs and plant bugs then separate cleanly.

Add `arbitration_rule_id` (which dispatch rule won) and `binding_constraint` (which limit is currently active). The latter would have flagged the 33-second rail saturation as a first-class event rather than something you notice by squinting at a constant.

## Tier 3 — the causal input

GridSignal's entire thesis is queue-telemetry-as-leading-indicator, and there is no queue telemetry in the log.

- Per-job records: `job_id`, `submit_ts`, `start_ts`, `requested_gpus`, `hardware_profile_id`, `estimated_duration`
- Aggregates: `jobs_pending`, `jobs_running`, `gpus_allocated`
- `job_phase` — train / checkpoint / eval / idle
- `checkpoint_active` boolean with `checkpoint_start_ts`

`job_phase` matters more than it looks. Your checkpoint-valley classifier has precise thresholds (15% drop, 5–30 s, 90% recovery within 45 s), but scoring precision and recall requires ground-truth labels. Without a phase column there is no way to know whether the classifier is firing on real checkpoints or on noise.

## Tier 4 — complete the physical chains

**Cooling.** You log chilled water *supply* temp and flow but not return temp, so heat actually removed is uncomputable. Add `chw_return_temp_c` and you get Q = ṁ·c𝑝·ΔT as a free cross-check against IT load — the cooling model becomes self-validating. Also add `ambient_dry_bulb_c` and `wet_bulb_c`, `condenser_water_temp_c`, `chiller_stage_count`, and `compressor_vfd_pct`. Ambient is the confounder that made the rising-COP-with-falling-CHWS observation ambiguous; with it logged, that becomes a decidable question.

**BESS.** Add `bess_current_a`. With voltage and power already present you get P = V·I as a three-way redundancy check — that alone would have caught the decoupled terminal voltage immediately. Also `bess_power_limit_charge_mw` / `_discharge_mw` (separate limits), `bess_soc_limit_min/max`, `bess_cell_temp_c`.

**Turbine.** Add `gt_fuel_flow_mmbtu_h` and `gt_inlet_air_temp_c`. Heat rate is currently derived, so nothing validates it — with measured fuel flow, heat rate becomes a checkable identity instead of a model output. Inlet air temp matters because GT output is strongly ambient-dependent and that dependency is currently invisible. Add `gt_ramp_rate_limit_mw_s` and `gt_mode` (offline/starting/synchronizing/online) so you can tell when a limiter is actually binding.

**Thermal lag.** Add rack `inlet` *and* `outlet` temps. The dual-clock model's whole observable signature is the phase relationship between them and the cooling response, and you currently have neither end of it modeled.

## Tier 5 — metadata that makes the log self-describing

Diagnosing the SOC sign error required me to infer a 200 MWh pack capacity from the data. That should have been a lookup.

- `bess_capacity_mwh`, `gt_rating_mw`, `solar_nameplate_mw` — nameplate constants in a header or sidecar
- Declared sign conventions (is negative discharge or charge?) and per-column units
- `channel_source` per column: measured / derived / modeled / noise-injected
- `scenario_id`, `scenario_tick`, `rng_seed`, `schema_version`
- A monotonic tick counter alongside wall-clock time
- `sample_quality` / `stale` flags

The `channel_source` tag is quietly valuable — it would have answered "is this commanded or measured?" without any inference at all.

## On sampling

Given the aliasing risk we just discussed, consider a two-rate scheme rather than raising everything: keep 1 Hz for slow plant state, and log IT load plus GPU power at 10 Hz or faster. Everything mechanical and thermal has time constants in the tens of seconds and gains nothing from oversampling.
