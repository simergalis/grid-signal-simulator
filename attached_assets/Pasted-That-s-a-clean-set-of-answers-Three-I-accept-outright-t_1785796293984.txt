Ramp rounding — accepted, but R7 is now unmeasurable

Your arithmetic is right: 0.020 MW clamp plus ±0.005 rounding on each of two samples gives a 0.030 MW apparent maximum, hence 0.3 MW/s measured against 0.2 actual.

But that means my own acceptance criterion R7 — measured down-ramp within ±5% of r_asset_down — can't be evaluated. At 0.15 MW/s the per-tick delta is 0.015 MW, and 2dp quantization is 0.01 MW. The measurement error is ~67% of the signal.

Cheapest fix is measuring the rate over a window rather than per-tick: 50 ticks at 0.2 MW/s gives 1.00 MW total against 0.01 MW quantization, a 1% error. No schema change. Reword R7 as mean ramp rate over any 5-second window. If you'd rather fix it at the source, 4dp on the power channels costs almost nothing at 600 rows.

Also worth confirming the corollary now that the clamp is at spec: BESS should be covering close to 100% of the 0.75 s pulse. That's checkable as corr(bess_power_mw, it_load_mw) approaching −1, with the balance residual dropping well below 13.6 MW. If it doesn't, something else is absorbing the step.

tau_gpu — accepted

96.3% predicted, 28.9 of 30 observed. One note: with τ = 0.06 s and dt = 0.1 s, the filter's time constant is shorter than the sample interval, so the smoothing is essentially invisible at 10 Hz. That's fine — it's the right physics and it kills the infinite-slope edge — but if you ever want to show the edge shape, it needs a faster capture. Not a defect.

TC-03 — fix the test, not the threshold

2.08% against a 2% threshold with 1.38% of that coming from noise means this test will pass and fail randomly across CI runs. A flaky acceptance test is worse than a missing one, because it teaches people to re-run until green.

Two changes, both cheap, and I'd do both:

Zero the noise for this test. TC-03 asserts a deterministic convergence property of the thermal filter. Stochastic COP and pump noise aren't part of what's under test — they're contamination. Run it in a noise-disabled mode.

Settle to 7τ rather than 5τ. At 5τ you have 0.67% genuine lag residual eating a third of your 2% budget. At 7τ that drops to 0.09%. The extra sim time is free.

With both, the measured gap should land near 0.1% and the threshold stops being marginal. Task #184 is the right home for it.

The forecast finding is better news than it looks

forecast_mw = sum(g.target_output_mw() for g in state.gpu_modules), with _node_counts populated exclusively through apply_signal() from WorkloadSignal events — that means the forecast is queue-derived via the Section 4 formula. It is not extrapolating measured draw.

So this is Branch B, not Branch A. IP claim 1 is structurally implemented and F3 should pass today. The defect is a startup ordering bug, not an architectural one. That's a materially different position than where we were two turns ago, and worth recording as such.

One push, though. I'd question "a single node with a near-zero profile weight." Working backwards, 0.13 MW at PUE_base 1.03 implies ~126 kW of rated node power — that's not a small node, it's roughly twelve enterprise_8gpu_air nodes. And the Forecast Quality chart showed the value climbing from ~0.10 to ~0.38 MW across 15 seconds, which reads as _node_counts being populated progressively rather than being empty-then-full.

That matters because it splits the diagnosis again:

If forecast_mw converges to the declared allocation after the startup gap closes, the defect is purely a race and the 0.13 MW panel was a screenshot taken 15 s into a run.
If it settles at ~0.38 MW for a 19.96 MW job, there's a separate magnitude bug underneath the race, and fixing the ordering won't touch it.

So task #183 should capture the full forecast_mw trajectory to steady state and compare the settled value against nodes_declared × kW_i × PUE_base — not just the value at first broadcast. That single comparison distinguishes the two.
