The bars are measured against expected, not nameplate

This is the choice that makes the screen work. If each bar were a fraction of its 1.00 MW rating, then on a hazy morning all twenty would sit at 40% and the display would scream problem when nothing is wrong. Measured against expected output at current irradiance, a healthy bank reads full regardless of weather — so the only short bars are the ones that are actually underperforming. Weather moves the whole plant total; it doesn't move the bars.

Grouped by feeder, not sorted by severity

The instinct is to sort faults to the top. I'd resist it here. Sorting scatters bank-06 through bank-10 into an arbitrary list where five separate failures and one feeder failure look identical. Keeping physical order makes the block of five contiguous banks unmissable — and that contiguity is the diagnosis. Five inverters don't independently fail in the same minute. Something upstream did.

Four feeders of five banks each, with subtotals, gives you the grouping for free and keeps the list navigable.

Three states, not two

bank-04 at 0.62 MW is not the same situation as bank-08 at zero, and both would render as "not green" in a two-state design. Degraded banks are still producing and are counted at what they can actually do — excluding them overstates the shortfall and triggers curtailment you didn't need. Each degraded row carries its reason, because "6 strings open" dispatches a technician while "soiling 9%" schedules a wash and "inverter derate" may clear on its own when the afternoon cools.

The footer changes a number, not just a label

This is the part that isn't cosmetic. The current model computes N−1 as the largest single bank — 0.87 MW. With feeder structure, the credible single contingency is a whole feeder, 4.06 MW. That's a 4.7× understatement of the exposure the reserve is sized against, and it's silent, because the check passes comfortably either way until the day it doesn't.

Fixing it means adding a feeder_id to the bank config and computing N−1 over groups. That's the change I'd make before any of the layout work.

One honesty note on "no comms"

I labelled feeder B as no comms rather than out, because that's usually what you actually know. The June 14 event in your loss table is exactly this: a comms failure presented as a generation failure and cost 2.1 MW of curtailed compute while the array was still producing. The UI should say what it observed — telemetry stopped — rather than assert generation stopped, and the reserve check should treat it conservatively while flagging that the two are being conflated.

For the layman reading this screen: fifteen of twenty working, five knocked out together by one fault, three running weak. That sentence is the top line, and everything below it is the evidence.
