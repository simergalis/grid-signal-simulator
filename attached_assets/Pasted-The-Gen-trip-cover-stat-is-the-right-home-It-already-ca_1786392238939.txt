GEN-TRIP COVER
What N-1 means here

This site is islanded — there is no utility feed to lean on. "N-1" asks one question: if the largest generator running right now tripped offline this instant, could everything on the bus still be served by what's left?

The MW shed figure is the answer when it's no. It is the size of the gap that would remain after every surviving generator ran up to its ceiling. It is not a count of servers, and it is not a prediction that anything will trip.

Read it as: this much compute draw would have to pause to bring demand back in line with supply.

At 0.00 MW the surviving fleet absorbs the loss on its own and no compute is at risk. As it climbs, the site is running closer to its own edge — usually because load ramped faster than generation was staged, or because a unit is already out.

Why megawatts and not nodes. The obvious question is how many servers that is. We don't show a number, because the honest answer changes every few seconds. A node's draw depends on what its job is doing at that moment — a training run pulls far more while computing than while the network exchanges results — and the cooling that follows that draw arrives about 90 seconds behind it. A node count would look precise and be stale by the time you read it. Megawatts is what the electrical system actually responds to, so megawatts is what we show.

Four things to fix before this ships

1. The ÷PUE term in your conversion is probably wrong, and wrong in the unsafe direction. Spec §4 splits overhead deliberately: PUE_base is instant (distribution, conversion, UPS) and α(t) is the lagged cooling term at Δt_thermal. Your formula uses a single "PUE," which collapses that split back together. Working from the tile figures: 19.96 MW ÷ 1,900 nodes = 10.5 kW/node at the rack boundary, and 4.59 ÷ 19.96 gives α_max ≈ 0.23. So the cooling-inclusive divisor is 12.9 kW, giving ~77 nodes/MW — not 95. Your 95 is the rack-boundary number, computed with PUE_base only.

Which one is correct depends on where deficit_mw is measured. If it's at the site bus, dividing by a cooling-inclusive PUE assumes cooling relief you don't get for 90 seconds — you'd under-shed during precisely the window the shed exists to cover. Worth confirming the boundary before this number appears anywhere, including internally.

2. The formula and the profile name shouldn't be in operator-visible text. max(0, deficit_mw − headroom_surviving_mw) is reserve-evaluation criteria and rated_kw_per_node × PUE with enterprise_8gpu_air is hardware-profile methodology. Both are in the do-not-disclose set pending IP review, and a demo shown to Jeff Baer is disclosure. Keep them in Log and Trace or an internal-only expansion.

3. Make it static copy. The prose above should be authored strings in the tile component, not narration. Only the MW value binds, and it binds from computed truth — no arithmetic in the TSX layer.

4. Header state consistency. The stat currently reads "N-1 ready." When shed > 0 it needs to flip to the MW figure and lose the "ready" label, or the header contradicts the panel a click away.
