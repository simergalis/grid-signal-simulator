Three numbers I'd challenge

GT ramp clamp at 4.0 MW/s. Section 7.1 specifies r_asset = 0.2 MW/s, and Phase 11.3b proposed 0.15 for down-ramp. 4.0 is 20× the spec value, and it appears to be a chosen constant rather than a traced one. That the 99th percentile also sits at 4.0 tells you the clamp binds essentially every tick — the command signal is still asking for far more, so sub-band content is still reaching the dispatch path. The clamp rate-limits the symptom.

The more useful experiment: set it to the spec's 0.2 MW/s. The turbine will then be unable to follow the 0.75 s pulse at all, and the BESS will have to cover 100% of it. That's not a failure — it's the architecturally correct outcome under Section 28.1, and it demonstrates the band separation rather than blurring it.

tau_gpu at 0.3 s against a 0.2 s pulse. The high state runs 26.7% duty on a 0.746 s period — roughly 0.2 s wide. A first-order filter with τ = 0.3 s reaches only 1 − e^(−0.2/0.3) ≈ 49% of the step before the pulse ends. So the pulse amplitude is now roughly halved by filter attenuation, not by physics. If peak-to-peak looks better in the new capture, some of that improvement is artifact. τ = 0.06 s (the Phase 10 default) reaches 96% and preserves the amplitude while still killing the infinite-slope edge.

corr(mech, it) = 0.011 doesn't demonstrate the 90 s lag. With a 90 s onset delay and a 60 s capture, cooling never responds inside the window — so near-zero correlation is what you'd get from a correctly-lagged model and from a disconnected one. It satisfies TC-02 (P_cooling = 0 at t₀ + 60 s) and proves nothing beyond it.

TC-03 is the test that distinguishes them: hold a constant job for ≥ Δt_thermal + 5τ and confirm convergence to α_max × P_compute within 2% of asymptote. That needs a capture of several hundred seconds, not 60. Until it runs, "cooling lag implemented" and "cooling unplugged" are observationally identical.
