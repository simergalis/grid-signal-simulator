Suggested resolution

Split the term rather than choosing between the two positions:

Channel	Meaning	Drives frequency?
asset_delivery_error_mw	(turbine_out − gt_setpoint) + (bess_out − bess_setpoint) — real shortfall	Yes
model_error_mw	Everything unaccounted after all physical terms	No

Forcing becomes frequency_forcing_mw + asset_delivery_error_mw. That's identical to today's numerical behaviour when the model is correct, preserves your physics argument intact, and gives I4 something it can actually assert against.

The honest difficulty: computing a genuine model_error_mw requires an independent accounting of every physical term, and if it's derived as "balance residual minus everything else" it becomes the slack variable D5 was written to prevent. Options, roughly in order of effort:

Independent energy accounting — track cumulative energy in and out per asset, compare against integrated power. A units or double-counting bug shows up as divergence over time. Strongest, most work.
Invariant assertions instead of a channel — assert per-asset that output lies within physical bounds, that PUE terms aren't double-counted, that cumulative charge matches SoC change. Catches most real bugs without needing a residual at all.
Accept that model error isn't separately observable and rename the channel to match what it measures.

The third is defensible if documented. What isn't defensible is a channel named model_error_mw that no longer means model error, because the next person to read it will assume it's a fault detector.

What I'd send back

Not a rework of 13.2 — the decomposition, the tests, and D1–D5 are sound. Two targeted changes:

Rename model_error_mw → asset_delivery_error_mw and update the docstring to say it captures physical shortfall, which is why it correctly participates in frequency forcing.
Change the swing equation input to frequency_forcing_mw + asset_delivery_error_mw rather than _balance_residual_mw. Numerically identical today; explicit about what drives frequency and what doesn't; and it leaves room for a real model_error_mw later without touching the control path again.

Then revise I4 in the addendum to test the surviving distinction: inject a fault that produces balance residual without asset delivery shortfall — a load-model error, say — and assert frequency doesn't move. That's the property worth protecting, and it's still testable.

One smaller note: D3 passing at "< 0.5% of site load" for model_error_mw in both modes is expected under the current formula, since healthy assets track their setpoints. It's not evidence the channel would catch a modelling bug — the depleted-BESS test is, and that's the one to keep pointing at.
